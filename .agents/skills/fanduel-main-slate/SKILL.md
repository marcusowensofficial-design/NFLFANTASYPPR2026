## 3. The 4 Golden Strategic Directives

### A. The "Rushing QB Ceiling Inversion" Axiom
* **The Public Mistake:** When an elite dual-threat QB (Josh Allen, Lamar Jackson, Jalen Hurts) faces a ferocious pass rush (top-8 defensive line), consensus models and the casual public downgrade the QB as a "bad matchup," driving their ownership down to sub-5%.
* **The Mathematical Reality:** Fierce edge rushers breakdown pocket passing, **forcing mobile QBs to scramble for high-value rushing yards and goal-line sneaks**. 
* **The Strategy:** When an elite dual-threat QB is projected under 8% ownership due to perceived matchup difficulty in a dome or high-total game, **aggressively stack them**. Pair them with their primary intermediate safety valve (TE or Slot WR) who benefits from quick checkdowns against the blitz.

### B. "Good Chalk" vs. "Bad Chalk"
* **Never fade good chalk just to be contrarian:**
  * **Good Chalk (Eat It):** 
    * Bellcow Running Backs with guaranteed 20+ touch workloads running behind top-5 offensive lines in games with 48.0+ totals (e.g., Jahmyr Gibbs at 45%).
    * Alpha WRs with $\ge 28\%$ target share facing secondaries ranked bottom-5 in DvP (e.g., Chris Olave at 30%).
    * *Why:* Their median-to-ceiling distribution is so concentrated that fading them usually spots the field 30+ points.
  * **Bad Chalk (Fade It):**
    * Pocket QBs with high ownership (>20%) in non-shootout games.
    * Committee RBs priced above $7,000 who depend entirely on multiple TDs without 3-down passing game work.
* **The Differentiation Rule:** Eat the 2 high-floor chalk pieces (Gibbs + Olave), and create your slate-breaking leverage through a **sub-5% QB/TE primary stack** and a **sub-8% cheap secondary bring-back**.

### C. The Touchdown Monopolization Principle
* When game-stacking a high-total shootout (e.g., BUF @ HOU 36-31), do not just roster pass-catchers. 
* Combine the **QB + Pass Catcher from Team A** with the **RB1 + WR1 from Team B**. 
* This captures 100% of the game's touchdowns:
  * Team A scores via QB passes and QB scrambles.
  * Team B scores via goal-line rushes and WR1 deep balls.
  * You monopolize 60+ real-life points without needing the opposing QB.

### D. Overcoming the "Disgust Fallacy"
* Trust **Vegas game totals (47.0+) over public brand disgust**.
* When a low-reputation team (like Carolina) is featured in a game with an O/U of 47.5+, it means Vegas expects points. 
* If that team possesses a dead-last defensive front (PFF #31), attack it with the opposing bellcow RB (D'Andre Swift), and correlate it with the cheap underdog target vacuum (Jalen Coker) who benefits from garbage time dropbacks.

1. **Vegas Total Tiering:**
   * Isolate the top 4 game totals on the slate ($\ge 46.5$ O/U) and verify dome/weather conditions.

   * Scan dual-threat QBs with projected ownership $<8\%$. If one plays in a competitive, high-pace environment, make them your primary stack.
3. **PFF Trench Mismatches:**

   * Cross-reference WR DvP ranks (Rank 1-8 softest). Identify shadow CB matchups and determine which secondary WR/Slot absorbs the vacated target volume.
5. **Enforce the 3-Game Matrix Geometry:**
   * Lineup must feature: 1 Primary Game Stack (3-4 players) + 2 Opposing Mini-Stacks (2 players each) + 1 Cheap D/ST.

6. **Cumulative Ownership Budgeting:**
   * Balance 1-2 pieces of high-floor Good Chalk (30-45% own) with at least 3 high-ceiling leverage pieces ($<8\%$ own).
